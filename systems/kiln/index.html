kiln · design system

A page that keeps
its notes in the margin.

Kiln sets long-form work the way a magazine does: cream stock, warm ink, one terracotta moment per screen, and asides that live beside the line they belong to rather than interrupting it. Nothing in this system casts a shadow.

Practice

How the margin works

The rail is a real outer margin, not a callout box parked inside the text.

Depth comes from the surface shifting, not from the surface lifting. The rule that decides every component in this system.

A pull-quote has to sit in ordinary block flow to work — the paragraphs around it wrap because it is a float, and floats are ignored by flex and grid containers. That is what .prose is for: a plain passage of text where the quote can do its job.

Everything else in this section is a grid, so the quote lives up here with the prose rather than down among the steps. On a page with no margin rail you can add .bleed to push it out into the outer margin; on this page the rail already has notes in it, so it stays inside the column.

01

Set the column

Wrap the reading column and its rail in .col. The column is capped at 680px and the rail takes the remaining 220px, so running text lands between 65 and 72 characters without any per-page tuning.1

02

Anchor the notes

Give each .sidenote an id and point a .noteref at it. On a wide screen the note is pulled level with the line that references it; notes can never overlap because each one is placed below the previous note's bottom edge.2

03

Let it collapse

Below 1080px the rail drops under the column and the notes lay out as a row. Below 640px tables restack as cards and the body text grows to 18px — reading on a small screen is harder, so the type gets larger, not smaller.3

Emphasis

Weight, not italic. This is an em — it renders at weight 500 in terracotta-deep, because italics on this stock read as an apology.

Hover

Colour and border only. Nothing translates, nothing scales. A printed page does not float when you point at it.

Panels

One surface, no border at rest — a hairline appears on hover. That is the entire interaction vocabulary for a container.

On the measure. 680px is the long-form cap. App shells use the full 1180px --shell instead; only prose is held to the narrow column.

On alignment. Anchoring re-runs after document.fonts.ready — webfonts land after first paint and change every measurement underneath them.

On collapse. Stacked table rows print their column head from data-label, so consumer tables must set it.

Inverse band

One dark band per page, used to end a section rather than to decorate one.

The band flips --paper-deep against --paper. In dark mode both invert, so the interlude stays an interlude instead of disappearing into the page.
