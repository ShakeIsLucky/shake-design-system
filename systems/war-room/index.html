The War Room Wall

The page is a wall. Every printed thing lives on paper — briefs, typed slips, index cards — pinned or taped to a deep spruce ground. Reading text never sits on the wall; the wall is the margin, never the page.

One stylesheetOne moduleZero buildNothing floats

Studio note · the rule

Emphasis is analog. Highlighter, sharpie and tape do the work bold text and badges do everywhere else.

The Brief

the printed ground — where all reading lives

A brief is paper. It carries the argument in full sentences, at a comfortable measure, in a printed grotesque — nothing to decode.

Field note · on paper

Body copy sets in Archivo at a sixty-to-seventy-character measure. The one thing we never do is set a paragraph directly on the wall. The surface has to win over its ground, always.

When a line is verbatim and load-bearing, it earns the highlighter. When it is a number that decides something, it earns a circle.

What each voice is for

Oswald is the printed header. Archivo is the brief you are reading. Courier is a typed transcript slip. Permanent Marker is the sharpie in someone's hand — annotations only, never a heading, never a paragraph.

Sketch · the stack

Margin note · tape or pin

Every artifact hangs by one of two things — a strip of tape or a single pin. Nothing floats in space; the shadow underneath is a real cast, not a glow.

Margin note · the wobble

A hand pinned this, so it sits a degree or two off true. Wobble is craft, not mess — capped at plus-or-minus one-point-two degrees.

Voices on the Wall

chips, tags, marker — the annotation layer

Short labels can touch the wall directly — a printed chip, a scrawled marker line. Anything longer than a phrase goes back onto paper.

External vocab Sharpie Printed ink Highlighter

“a chip names the source; the marker names the feeling.”

The blue chip is reserved for external-product vocabulary only — a borrowed voice, never decoration.

The Deck

flip a card for the note behind it

Studio note · the deck is the device

A deck of flippable index cards is the one card grid the world allows. No icon-tile grids — the cards flip because index cards flip.

Studio note · the gotcha

The inner wrapper is display block with an explicit height. Inline spans collapse the absolute-positioned faces — a lesson learned once.

The Launch List

index card, sharpie checks, saved on the device

Interactives have to be things a wall could hold. A checklist gets checked; the marks are red sharpie, and they stay checked next time you visit.

Field note · interaction grammar

Cards flip, checklists get checked, the agenda earns marker ticks as you pass each chapter. Nothing here is decorative motion — every moving part serves memory.
